# Community Acceptance of Biolarviciding

## Background

A community survey examined awareness, perceived effectiveness, willingness to participate and overall acceptance of biolarviciding for malaria vector control. The report is a synthetic fixture created for EvidenceCast deployment testing; it contains no personal data and must not be presented as a new research result.

## Findings

Awareness of biolarviciding was high among surveyed residents, but understanding of how and where larvicides are applied varied across communities.

Residents generally viewed biolarviciding as an effective complement to established malaria-control measures rather than a replacement for bed nets, environmental management or timely treatment.

Willingness to support the intervention was strongest when implementation included clear public information, trusted local leadership and opportunities for residents to ask questions.

Overall acceptance was positive, although continued community engagement was considered necessary to address misconceptions and maintain confidence.

## Communication implications

Public-facing media should explain what biolarviciding does, where it is applied and how it fits alongside other malaria-control measures. Claims should remain linked to the approved source text, and uncertainty should not be replaced with invented statistics.
